P.O.G. Part 2: Locke & Hobbes and Human Nature

State of Nature A hypothetical state where there are no laws, governments, or rules. People have complete freedom but no guaranteed protection.
Social Contract An agreement where citizens give up some freedoms in exchange for protection from the government.
Sovereign A supreme ruler or ultimate authority holding political power.
Tabula Rasa John Locke's idea that people are born with a "blank slate" and shaped by experiences.
Natural Rights Fundamental rights all human beings are born with (such as life, liberty, and property).

1) What were Hobbes' and Locke's beliefs about human nature?

Hobbes believed people were naturally selfish and chaotic in a state of nature. Locke believed people were born as a blank slate (Tabula Rasa) and possessed natural rights.

2) Why did they believe government was necessary?

To prevent chaos and protect people, their rights, and property from others.

3) What did each believe was the purpose/job of government?

Hobbes felt government needed total control to maintain order. Locke believed government existed solely to protect natural rights and serve the public.

P.O.G. Part 3: Types of Government

Autocracy: One person holds all power (Monarchy or Dictatorship).
Monarchy: King/Queen rules through family lineage.
Dictatorship: Single leader has absolute control over citizens' lives.
Oligarchy: Small group of people holds all political power.
Theocracy: Government recognizing God or divine authority as supreme rule.
Anarchy: Nobody is in control (or everyone is).
Direct Democracy: Citizens directly make laws and decisions.
Representative Democracy: Citizens elect leaders to make laws and govern.

Lesson 3: What is Republican Government?

Civic Virtue: Putting aside personal self-interests for the good of the community/country.
Common Good: Whatever helps the whole community rather than just one person.
Delegate: A representative chosen by citizens to act on their behalf.
Faction: Groups of interest that could divide citizens in a large republic.
Republic: A country where power is held by citizens electing representatives.

How did colonists teach civic virtue?

Parents taught children at home, teachers taught at school, through stories, and by designing public buildings similarly to ancient Roman buildings.

Example of Civic Virtue: Cincinnatus

Cincinnatus led Rome to victory in battle, gave up his dictator/military power, and returned to farming.

P.O.G. Part 4: Limiting Government

Constitution: Rulebook for government; explains how laws are made and limits government power.
Rule of Law: Everyone, including government leaders and the president, must follow the law.
Minority Rights: Protection of small or unpopular groups so the majority cannot treat them unfairly.
Consent of the Governed: Citizens give permission to be governed by exercising their right to vote.
Separation of Powers: Dividing government authority among branches (legislative, executive, judicial).
